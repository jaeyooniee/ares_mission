    Weather model · NASA Ames Mars GCM / EMARS

    The page reads web/data/weather_gale.json, built by python/emars_extract.py from the public EMARS v1.0 files (Penn State Data Commons). Only the bytes of the grid points around Gale are downloaded (HTTP range requests on the NetCDF-3 files), not the 1–5 GB files.

    What the data is

    EMARS (Ensemble Mars Atmosphere Reanalysis System, Greybush et al. 2019) runs the NASA Ames Mars GCM (github.com/nasa/AmesGCM) and assimilates TES and MCS temperature retrievals. It gives hourly ground and air temperature, surface pressure, winds and dust opacity for Mars Years 24–33. It is a record of past Mars years, not a forecast: no real-time Mars weather forecast exists yet. Real past years are used as scenarios: a clear season (MY32), a dusty season (MY29) and the 2007 global dust storm (MY28).

    Limits of the GCM and how we handle them

    Running the GCMFortran + MPI on Linux, hours to days per Mars year → we use the published EMARS output instead of running the model
    ResolutionOne grid cell is about 5° × 6° (≈ 300 × 350 km), so the whole 10 km map is inside one cell → weather is one value per hour for the whole map; it changes the plan through time. Spatial detail comes from the terrain (wind-exposed ridges, hazards in poor visibility)
    Not a forecastValues are the weather of a past Mars year at that season → three real scenarios
    Thin airAir density ≈ 0.02 kg/m³: even 20 m/s pushes very little → wind matters through blowing dust and sand on exposed ground; dust and temperature drive the budget
    Height mismatchThe GCM's smoothed ground is ≈ 3 km above the Gale floor → pressure moved hydrostatically to the habitat height (about 10–15 % above Curiosity REMS); wind scaled from the lowest model level (~36 m) to 2 m (×0.65)

    How weather enters the route and GO / NO-GO

    Dust τ (visibility)Two visibility classes (on foot τ ≥ 0.9 / 1.5, rover τ ≥ 1.5 / 3) lower the slope limit, make slopes cost more, keep a margin from blocked ground and slow travel. A* re-plans on that cost map. τ ≥ τ limit → NO-GO
    Wind (2 m)Above level 1 (on foot 7 m/s, rover 10 m/s) the most exposed 20 % of the ground costs ×2.5; above level 2 (10 / 15 m/s) the most exposed 10 % is blocked → a sheltered route. Wind ≥ wind limit → NO-GO
    TemperatureSuit heater 1.5 W per °C below −40 °C (cooling 3 W per °C above +10 °C), rover cabin heating 25 W per °C below +5 °C, hour by hour from the battery
    Time of dayOn foot: depart after 06:00 and be back before 18:00 local mean solar time; an EVA longer than the 8 h limit is NO-GO

    The route is chosen with the worst weather inside the expected EVA window, then the budget is integrated with the weather of each hour along the way. Every departure hour of the 3 sols is evaluated ("departure-time A*"). All thresholds are team assumptions. Source: Greybush, S. J., et al. (2019), Geosci. Data J. 6, 137–150; dataset doi:10.18113/D3W375.

    Mission planner and scheduled missions

    Science Objectives → Next: the best departures for Habitat → A → B → Habitat are listed, plus split options (A in one EVA, B in the next good departure) when one EVA is too long for the O₂ / battery or the 8 h limit. Pick an option and press Confirm (only when the badge shows GO); it is saved under SCHEDULED, where it can be replayed on the map.

    Data used in this build

    Elevation · slopeHiRISE DEM of Gale (1 m source, shown at 16 m)
    ImageryUSGS HiRISE colour mosaic of Gale (colour strips fused with the 25 cm red mosaic) — processed colour
    Routes · budgetPlanned in the page: A* on the slope / cost grid of the DEM, with the team pipeline's models (Tobler walking speed × suit factor, Pandolf metabolic O₂ at Mars gravity; rover speed and Wh/km)
    MineralsMRO CRISM summary parameters
    Scientific valueTeam science.py: HiRISE DEM (geology, reachability), CRISM summary parameters (life, water), THEMIS thermal inertia (resources), Curiosity traverse (novelty), scored against NASA Moon to Mars objectives
    Ground truth (Gale)Curiosity CheMin drill sites: NASA PDS CheMin RDR and PDS Analyst's Notebook (clay wt% from Thorpe et al. 2022); Curiosity traverse: NASA MMGIS. Shown in LAYERS on the map only, as reference: not used in route scoring
    Custom sitesBuilt in your browser from your own files with the same rules as the built-in sites (DEM; optional image, CRISM MTRDR, THEMIS; rover traverse from NASA MMGIS). Nothing is uploaded; the site is kept in this browser and can be downloaded as a package
    WeatherEMARS v1.0 reanalysis (NASA Ames Mars GCM + TES/MCS assimilation, Penn State), hourly point timeline at Gale for 3 real Mars-year scenarios — see Info → Weather model
    Not in this buildSoft soil (THEMIS), boulders, live space weather (DONKI), radiation, rover photos, real-time Mars weather forecast (none exists yet)
